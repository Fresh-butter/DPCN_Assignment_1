## Pipeline / data

- Loaded via `common/contract.py`, the single shared data contract:
  `Survey_Results_UC.csv` → Likert 1-5 → drop rows with >30 of 60
  missing → **87 respondents**. Dropping any row with a single missing answer
  would leave 85 and cost two respondents for no reason, so the >30 rule is
  used instead. `src/load.py` only re-indexes the result 0..86, since this
  network's nodes are statements and respondent identity is never used.
- 60 items kept: T01-T15, E01-E15, S01-S15, V01-V15.
- Missing-data sensitivity check: pairwise-complete correlation vs.
  mean-imputed z-scored correlation differ negligibly (mean |Δr| = 0.0033,
  max = 0.069). Missingness here is light and not systematic enough to
  matter. Pairwise-complete correlation used as primary. (`src/build.py`)
- Raw response distribution confirms the ceiling effect directly:
  **80% of all 5220 answers are Agree/Strongly Agree**, only 62 are
  missing/"No Comments". Per-item means cluster tightly between 3.5 and 4.5;
  only a handful of items (led by E02 and E03, the traditionalist-pedagogy
  pair) sit near or below the neutral midpoint. (`fig01_construction.png`)

## Distributions

- **Correlation distribution vs. permutation null** (`fig01_construction.png`):
  the observed |r| distribution across all 1770 pairs sits visibly to the
  right of the null generated by independently permuting each item, providing
  direct visual evidence that the network isn't built on noise. The FDR-significant
  subset (402 pairs, red) is exactly the right tail of the observed
  distribution, cut where it stops separating from the null (~|r|=0.27).
- **Degree**: mean 13.4, range 0-32, 2 isolates. No power-law hub, which is
  unsurprising at 60 nodes. E03 is the one statement with a large negative
  degree (8); every other statement has at most 1-2 negative edges.
- **Node strength** is mildly bimodal: a low-strength group (0-3, mostly
  T-block) and a broader higher-strength spread (5-12, dominated by S/V),
  consistent with the hub ranking. Both are summarised in the hub panel of
  `fig03_structure.png` rather than given their own figures.

## Network construction (edge-rule sensitivity)

- Primary rule: permutation-null + Benjamini-Hochberg FDR (q=0.05) on
  per-pair empirical p-values → 402 edges (391 positive, 11 negative),
  density 0.227, 2 isolated nodes (T08, T09). Smallest |r| retained is 0.274.
- A first attempt used a family-wise max-statistic permutation threshold
  (alpha=0.01, i.e. control the max |r| across all 1770 pairs). This gave
  |r| > 0.50 and kept zero negative edges, which would have erased the
  entire point of a signed network. Switched to per-edge FDR instead.
- Sensitivity check: fixed |r| > 0.30 cutoff → 316 edges (312 positive, 4
  negative), density 0.179, 4 isolates. The fixed threshold is markedly
  less generous to negative edges (4 vs 11). Construction choice matters
  most exactly where the interesting signed structure lives.
  (`tables/table00_edge_rule_sensitivity.csv`, `fig02_signed_network.png`)

## Communities vs. the survey's own T/E/S/V design

- Louvain on the positive-edge backbone finds 8 communities, i.e. agreement
  with the survey's own T/E/S/V labels is weak, NMI = 0.277.
- The class does not organize its opinions along the survey's own
  Technology/Education/Society/Environment axes. E.g. community 4 mixes 5
  Education + 8 Society + 2 Environment items; community 3 mixes items from
  all four blocks. (`fig03_structure.png`, `tables/table02*.csv`)

## Hubs and isolated statements

- Highest total agreement-strength (hubs): S09 (inclusive university
  environment), V07 (protecting biodiversity), S14 (ethical
  leadership), V09, V10 - broad, generically pro-social /
  pro-environmental consensus statements are the ones that best predict the
  rest of the survey.
- Fully isolated (no significant correlation with anything): T08
  ("AI-assisted diagnosis should become routine in healthcare") and T09
  ("Autonomous vehicles will ultimately make transportation safer"). These
  two specific-technology-application items are opinion outliers with no

  (`fig03_structure.png`, `tables/table03_hub_ranking.csv`)

## Structural balance / frustration

- Only 11/402 edges (2.7%) are negative, but they are not scattered: 8 of
  the 11 negative edges touch a single item, E03 ("Class attendance should
  be compulsory for all courses"). E03 anti-correlates with a cluster of
  progressive-education / innovation items (E04, E07, E10, E11, E12, T06,
  S06), i.e. a coherent "traditionalist pedagogy vs. progressive pedagogy" axis,
  not noise.
- Closed-triad census: 0 unbalanced (frustrated) triangles out of 1298
  closed triads, i.e. every triangle that exists is locally balanced.
- Frustration index (minimum edge-sign flips for a perfect two-faction
  split, greedy local search, 50-300 restarts, robust to restart count):
  2 / 402 edges (0.5%). Since the triangle census is already perfectly
  balanced, these 2 "wrong" edges must sit on a longer signed cycle (4- or
  5-cycle) that the triangle-only check can't see. This is worth noting as a
  triangle-balance vs. cycle-balance distinction in the report.
- Null model (reshuffle which of the 402 edges carry the 11 negative signs,
  topology fixed): mean frustration 10.98 ± 0.16 → observed frustration of 2
  is 56 standard deviations below chance (z = -56.2). The class's
  disagreement is placed with striking internal coherence, essentially the
  opposite of "incoherent belief system."
  (`fig03_structure.png`, `tables/table05_balance_summary.csv`)
- Minority faction in the best 2-coloring (excluding T08/T09, whose
  degree-0 status makes any faction label for them arbitrary):
  T01, E02, E03 vs. the other 55 connected statements. Interpretation is that
  T01 ("AI will improve society more than create problems") sits opposite
  T12 ("Governments should introduce stricter regulations for AI", r=-0.28),
  tech-optimism trades off against a felt need for regulation. E02 and E03
  cluster together (r=+0.28) as the shared "traditionalist" minority view.
  (`tables/table04_factions.csv`)

### E02 / T10 reverse-coding check

- **E02** ("Traditional written examinations accurately measure a student's
  knowledge") is a genuine contrarian/minority-faction item, exactly as
  flagged. It anti-correlates with E01 (progressive pedagogy) and sits in
  the 3-item minority faction.
- **T10** ("Protection of personal data is more important than
  technological convenience") is not an outlier here. It sits solidly in
  the majority faction, correlating positively with T11, T12, T14, S05, S12,
  V10 (all regulation/privacy-conscious items). The flagged concern about
  reverse phrasing doesn't manifest as anti-correlation for T10 in this
  network, it reads as a coherent part of the "cautious about tech" cluster
  rather than an opposite of it.

## Dynamics

- **Altafini signed consensus** (`dx/dt = -L_signed x`, seeded with each
  item's real deviation from the Likert midpoint): mean |opinion| decays
  from 1.215 to 0.080 over t ∈ [0, 120], a slow decay toward zero rather
  than a stable split or fast collapse. This is the dynamical signature of
  the balance result above: a graph that is almost but not exactly
  structurally balanced (frustration 2/402) is predicted by Altafini's
  theorem to eventually decay to zero, and the decay rate should scale with
  how much frustration there is very little here, hence very slow decay.
  (`fig04_dynamics.png`)
- **Perturbation / opinion-leverage ranking** (nudge one item, measure
  propagated signed movement across the giant component at t=1): leverage
  is dominated by structural centrality within the dense positive backbone.
  Top: S09, V10, S14, V13, V15 (all 0.966-0.967). Bottom: T15 (0.233),
  T01 (0.253), E04 (0.340), E13 (0.399), E02 (0.416). At this time horizon,
  being well-connected matters much more for spreading influence than being a
  source of disagreement.
  (`fig04_dynamics.png`, `tables/table06_leverage_ranking.csv`)

### Leverage grouped by block — the direct link to the agreement network

(`fig04_dynamics.png`, `tables/table07_leverage_by_block.csv`)

| Block | n | mean leverage | SD | min | max |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Technology | 13 | 0.714 | 0.249 | 0.233 | 0.953 |
| Education | 15 | 0.762 | 0.235 | 0.340 | 0.963 |
| Society/Ethics | 15 | **0.933** | 0.054 | 0.785 | 0.967 |
| Environment | 15 | **0.957** | **0.014** | 0.914 | 0.967 |

(Technology shows 13, not 15: T08 and T09 are isolates and absent from the
giant component the dynamics run on.)

- **S and V dominate leverage, and the gap is large** — mean 0.93/0.96 against
  0.71/0.76 for T and E.
- **More striking than the means is the spread.** Environment items have an SD
  of just **0.014** — every single one of the 15 sits between 0.914 and 0.967.
  Technology's SD is **18x larger** (0.249), ranging from 0.233 to 0.953. The
  Environment block behaves as one tightly-coupled bloc in the opinion graph;
  Technology is a loose collection of statements that happen to share a label.
- **This is the cleanest cross-member confirmation in the project.** Member A,
  working from an entirely different graph (students, not statements),
  finds the two opinion blocs are separated almost entirely by V and S items
  (mean |z-gap| per item: V 0.986, S 0.835 vs E 0.536, T 0.434). Two
  independent constructions, same ordering of the four blocks: **V > S > E > T**.
