## Pipeline / data

- Loaded via `common/contract.py`, the shared data contract:
  `Survey_Results_UC.csv` → Likert 1-5 → drop rows with >30 of 60
  missing → **87 respondents**. Dropping any row with a single missing answer
  would leave 85 and lose two respondents for no good reason, so we use the >30 rule
  instead. `src/load.py` only re-indexes the result 0..86, since this
  network's nodes are statements and we never use respondent identity.
- 60 items kept: T01-T15, E01-E15, S01-S15, V01-V15.
- Missing-data sensitivity check: pairwise-complete correlation vs.
  mean-imputed z-scored correlation are nearly identical (mean |Δr| = 0.0033,
  max = 0.069). There's so little missing data here that the handling choice
  doesn't really matter. Used pairwise-complete as the primary. (`src/build.py`)
- Raw response distribution confirms the ceiling effect:
  **80.0% of the 5158 answers actually given are Agree/Strongly Agree**
  (4127 of 5158; 79.1% of all 5220 cells, since 62 are missing/"No Comments"). Per-item means cluster tightly between 3.5 and 4.5;
  only a handful of items (led by E02 and E03, the traditionalist-pedagogy
  pair) sit near or below the neutral midpoint. (`fig01_construction.png`)

## Distributions

- **Correlation distribution vs. permutation null** (`fig01_construction.png`):
  the observed |r| distribution across all 1770 pairs sits clearly to the
  right of the null generated by independently permuting each item, which is
  direct visual evidence that the network is picking up real signal.
  The FDR-significant subset (402 pairs, red) is the right tail of the observed
  distribution, cut where it stops separating from the null (~|r|=0.27).
- **Degree**: mean 13.4, range 0-32, 2 isolates. No power-law hub, which
  isn't surprising at only 60 nodes. E03 is the one statement with a large negative
  degree (8); every other statement has at most 1-2 negative edges.
- **Node strength** is mildly bimodal: a low-strength group (0-3, mostly
  T-block) and a broader higher-strength spread (5-12, dominated by S/V),
  which matches the hub ranking. Both are shown in the hub panel of
  `fig03_structure.png` rather than given their own figures.

## Network construction (edge-rule sensitivity)

- Primary rule: permutation-null + Benjamini-Hochberg FDR (q=0.05) on
  per-pair empirical p-values → 402 edges (391 positive, 11 negative),
  density 0.227, 2 isolated nodes (T08, T09). Smallest |r| retained is 0.274.
- We first tried a family-wise max-statistic permutation threshold
  (alpha=0.01, controlling the max |r| across all 1770 pairs). This gave
  |r| > 0.50 and kept zero negative edges, which would've defeated the
  whole purpose of building a signed network. So we switched to per-edge FDR.
- Sensitivity check: fixed |r| > 0.30 cutoff → 316 edges (312 positive, 4
  negative), density 0.179, 4 isolates. The fixed threshold keeps way fewer
  negative edges (4 vs 11). The construction choice matters most exactly
  where the interesting signed structure is.
  (`tables/table00_edge_rule_sensitivity.csv`, `fig02_signed_network.png`)

## Communities vs. the survey's own T/E/S/V design

- Louvain on the positive-edge backbone finds 8 communities — so the match
  with the survey's own T/E/S/V labels is weak, NMI = 0.277.
- Basically, the class doesn't organise its opinions along the survey's own
  Technology/Education/Society/Environment axes. E.g. community 4 mixes 5
  Education + 8 Society + 2 Environment items; community 3 mixes items from
  all four blocks. (`fig03_structure.png`, `tables/table02*.csv`)

## Hubs and isolated statements

- Highest total agreement-strength (hubs): S09 (inclusive university
  environment), V07 (protecting biodiversity), S14 (ethical
  leadership), V09, V10 — broad, feel-good consensus statements are the ones
  most strongly connected to the rest of the survey.
- Fully isolated (no significant correlation with anything): T08
  ("AI-assisted diagnosis should become routine in healthcare") and T09
  ("Autonomous vehicles will ultimately make transportation safer"). These
  two specific-technology items are opinion outliers with no

## Structural balance / frustration

- Only 11/402 edges (2.7%) are negative, but they're not random: 8 of
  the 11 negative edges touch a single item, E03 ("Class attendance should
  be compulsory for all courses"). E03 anti-correlates with a cluster of
  progressive-education / innovation items (E04, E07, E10, E11, E12, T06,
  S06) — a coherent "traditionalist pedagogy vs. progressive pedagogy" axis,
  not noise.
- Closed-triad census: 0 unbalanced (frustrated) triangles out of 1298
  closed triads — every triangle that exists is locally balanced.
- Frustration index (minimum edge-sign flips needed for a perfect two-faction
  split, greedy local search, 50-300 restarts, stable across restart counts):
  2 / 402 edges (0.5%). Since the triangle census is already perfect,
  these 2 "wrong" edges must be on a longer signed cycle (4- or
  5-cycle) that the triangle check can't see.
- Null model (reshuffle which of the 402 edges carry the 11 negative signs,
  keep topology fixed): mean frustration 10.98 ± 0.16 → observed frustration of 2
  is 56 standard deviations below chance (z = -56.2). The class's
  disagreement is placed with a lot of internal coherence — basically the
  opposite of a random or incoherent belief system.
  (`fig03_structure.png`, `tables/table05_balance_summary.csv`)
- Minority faction in the best 2-coloring (excluding T08/T09, which are
  isolated so their faction label is meaningless):
  T01, E02, E03 vs. the other 55 connected statements. Reading this:
  T01 ("AI will improve society more than create problems") sits opposite
  T12 ("Governments should introduce stricter regulations for AI", r=-0.28) —
  tech-optimism trades off against wanting regulation. E02 and E03
  cluster together (r=+0.28) as the shared "traditionalist" minority view.
  (`tables/table04_factions.csv`)

### E02 / T10 reverse-coding check

- **E02** ("Traditional written examinations accurately measure a student's
  knowledge") is a genuine contrarian/minority-faction item, exactly as
  flagged. It anti-correlates with E01 (progressive pedagogy) and sits in
  the 3-item minority faction.
- **T10** ("Protection of personal data is more important than
  technological convenience") is not an outlier here. It sits solidly in
  the majority faction, correlating positively with T11, T12, T14, S05, S12,
  V10 (all regulation/privacy items). The concern about it being
  reverse-phrased doesn't show up as anti-correlation — it reads as a
  natural part of the "cautious about tech" cluster rather than the opposite of it.

## Dynamics

- **Altafini signed consensus** (`dx/dt = -L_signed x`, seeded with each
  item's real deviation from the Likert midpoint): mean |opinion| decays
  from 1.215 to 0.080 over t ∈ [0, 120] — a slow decay toward zero rather
  than a stable split or fast collapse. This makes sense given the balance
  result above: a graph that is almost perfectly balanced (frustration
  2/402) is predicted by Altafini's theory to eventually decay to zero, and
  the decay rate scales with how much frustration there is — very little here,
  so very slow decay.
  (`fig04_dynamics.png`)
- **Perturbation / opinion-leverage ranking** (nudge one item, measure
  how much signed movement it propagates across the giant component at t=1): leverage
  is mainly determined by how central a statement is in the positive backbone.
  Top: S09, V10, S14, V13, V15 (all 0.966-0.967). Bottom: T15 (0.233),
  T01 (0.253), E04 (0.340), E13 (0.399), E02 (0.416). At this time horizon,
  being well-connected matters much more for spreading influence than being a
  source of disagreement.
  (`fig04_dynamics.png`, `tables/table06_leverage_ranking.csv`)

### Leverage grouped by block — the link to the agreement network

(`fig04_dynamics.png`, `tables/table07_leverage_by_block.csv`)

| Block | n | mean leverage | SD | min | max |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Technology | 13 | 0.714 | 0.249 | 0.233 | 0.953 |
| Education | 15 | 0.762 | 0.235 | 0.340 | 0.963 |
| Society/Ethics | 15 | **0.933** | 0.054 | 0.785 | 0.967 |
| Environment | 15 | **0.957** | **0.014** | 0.914 | 0.967 |

(Technology shows 13, not 15: T08 and T09 are isolates and not in the
giant component the dynamics run on.)

- **S and V dominate leverage, and the gap is large** — mean 0.93/0.96 against
  0.71/0.76 for T and E.
- **The spread is even more interesting than the means.** Environment items have an SD
  of just **0.014** — all fifteen fall between 0.914 and 0.967.
  Technology's SD is **18x larger** (0.249), ranging from 0.233 to 0.953. 
  Environment behaves as one tightly-coupled bloc in the opinion graph;
  Technology is a loose bunch of statements that happen to share a label.
- Member A, working from an entirely different graph (students, not
  statements), finds the two opinion blocs are separated almost entirely by V
  and S items (mean |z-gap| per item: V 0.986, S 0.835 vs E 0.536, T 0.434).
  Two different constructions, same ordering of the four blocks:
  **V > S > E > T**. This is quantified in `FINDINGS_CROSS.md` §1 (Spearman ρ = +0.784
  across the 58 statements both measures score).

## Reproducibility

`notebook.ipynb` runs end to end and reproduces every number here. Re-running writes
identical CSVs apart from last-digit floating-point noise in
`table06_leverage_ranking.csv`, which comes from `scipy.linalg.expm`/BLAS and is ~1e-16.
Both graphs are written to `gexf/` from the notebook, with block, community, degree,
strength and faction as node attributes.
